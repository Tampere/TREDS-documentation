# Accordion

<ComponentBadges name="Accordion" lang="en" />

An accordion splits content into expandable sections under headings. The user opens only the sections they need.

<ComponentLinks name="Accordion" lang="en" />

<Example name="accordion/Hero" lang="en" />

## When to use

Use an accordion when a page has several separate sections of content and users usually need only some of them, for example frequently asked questions or additional details.

**Don't use an accordion when**

- users usually need all the content – show it directly under subheadings
- the sections are parallel views of the same thing – use <ComponentRef name="Tabs" lang="en" />
- there's only one short paragraph – show it directly

## Variants and situations

### Spacing

`spacing` is required. `default` separates the sections with gaps and shadows, as in the example at the top of the page. `none` stacks the sections tightly.

<Example name="accordion/NoSpacing" lang="en" />

### Open and close texts

`openLabel` and `closeLabel` show a text at the right edge of the heading that changes with the state. The texts become part of the button's name, so a screen reader hears for example "Application details Show, button, collapsed".

<Example name="accordion/OpenCloseLabels" lang="en" />

### Several sections open

Users can open several sections at once. All sections start closed, and none can be opened in advance (#213). `onChange` reports the `value`s of the open sections.

### Disabled

`disabled` prevents opening the section and greys out its heading. Explain elsewhere why the content isn't available.

## Content guidelines

- Write the heading so users know what's in the section before opening it. Questions suit FAQs.
- Don't hide information every user needs, like deadlines, in an accordion.
- Keep the headings short so they fit on one line on a phone too.

## Accessibility

**The component handles**

- The heading is a native `<button>` with `aria-expanded`. Screen readers say whether the section is open.
- Enter and Space open and close the section. The up and down arrow keys move focus from heading to heading.
- An open section is a `region` named after its heading.
- A focused heading gets the TREDS focus ring.

**Your responsibility**

- The headings aren't heading elements, so screen reader users can't find the sections by browsing headings (#212). Put a real heading above the accordion that says what the sections are about.
- The content of closed sections isn't on the page, so the browser's find (Ctrl+F) doesn't find it.
- The open and close texts become part of the button's name. If you use them, keep them short.
- The heading's chevron is exposed to screen readers as an unnamed image (#212).

WCAG criteria: [1.3.1 Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/info-and-relationships.html), [2.1.1 Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/keyboard.html), [2.4.6 Headings and Labels](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/headings-and-labels.html), [4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/name-role-value.html)
